# Energy Consumption Analysis System
The Energy Consumption Analysis System is a Python-based analytical solution designed to transform structured electricity consumption records in Nigeria using a simulated data into meaningful insights around energy usage, electricity cost, customer segments, locations, monthly consumption patterns, and potential energy efficiency opportunities.

The system was developed using Python fundamentals, with electricity records represented as a list of dictionaries and analyzed through reusable functions, conditional logic, loops, data validation, customer classification, and an interactive menu-driven interface.

The project demonstrates how a structured programming approach can be applied to a practical energy-analysis problem: moving from raw customer records to calculated metrics, analytical patterns, customer attention flags, and management-oriented insights.

---

## Problem Statement

Despite Africa’s considerable energy resources, electricity access, reliability, and affordability remain major challenges, particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa. Nigeria illustrates this challenge, as electricity availability and consumption do not always translate into reliable access for all users. Differences in location, customer type, electricity costs, and outages can also influence consumption patterns.

Electricity consumption analysis is important for understanding how energy is being used across different customer groups, locations, and periods. Without structured analysis, it can be difficult to understand overall demand, compare locations and customer groups, identify high-consuming customers, or recognize cases that require further attention.

---
## Solution
The team engineered a robust, terminal based Python application designed to ingest, validate, process, and analyze a simulated dataset of 20 customer records spanning Lagos, Abuja, and Ibadan The system leverages procedural programming principles, modular functions, control structures, and custom algorithms to automate comprehensive energy audits, calculate financial costs relative to average incomes, rank top consumers, evaluate power outage impacts, and dynamically flag high attention accounts.

---

## Importance of Problem

The insights generated by this system address several critical areas in the energy sector:
* **Energy Planning:** Helps stakeholders evaluate regional demand distribution across major urban centers to guide infrastructure investment.
* **Energy Efficiency:** Identifies high-consumption entities that require immediate energy conservation protocols or equipment audits.
* **Renewable Energy Integration:** Cross-references consumption volume with renewable energy shares to highlight heavy consumers with low clean-energy adoption.
* **Affordability Assessment:** Computes electricity costs against standard income metrics to gauge economic burdens on different consumer groups.

## Data Set

20 simulated customer records, each representing one customer's monthly electricity activity.
Fields: customer_id, customer_name, customer_type, location, month, consumption_kwh, tariff_per_kwh, peak_consumption_kwh, renewable_share, outage_hours, payment_status.
Customer types: Residential, SME, Industrial, Hospital, School, Agricultural.
Locations: Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan.
Months covered: January–June.
This is a simulated dataset, embedded directly in the Python program as a list of dictionaries. It is not sourced from an official utility or regulator and should not be read as representing actual Nigerian electricity statistics.

##  Features

* **Interactive Main Menu System:** Powered by persistent `while` loops, providing users with 13 distinct navigational options to execute specific analytical queries.
  
* **Multi-Level Location and Customer Type Exploration:** Built using nested `while` loops that allow analysts to cross-examine specific locations and customer types (e.g., Lagos + SME) seamlessly without restarting the program.
  
* **Defensive Data Validation (`validate_data`):** Implements robust `try-except` blocks to scan datasets for missing IDs, duplicate entries, negative consumption figures, and invalid payment statuses before calculations occur.
  
* **Financial and Affordability Calculations:** Computes individual electricity usage costs and measures expenses against an assumed average Nigerian monthly income (₦150,000) to categorize financial burdens.
  
* **Dynamic Customer Classification and High-Attention Flagging:** Categorizes users into Low (<500 kWh), Medium (500–999 kWh), and High (> 1000 kWh) demand tiers while automatically flagging high-volume accounts with renewable shares under 15%.
  
* **Top 10 Consumer Ranking (Bubble Sort):** Employs a custom Bubble Sort algorithm to rank and display the highest consuming accounts with corresponding cost and renewable metrics.
  
* **Energy Efficiency Scoring:** Evaluates customer profiles across conservation grades, clean energy shares, and power outage metrics to output operational efficiency ratings.
  
* **Outage vs. Consumption Evaluation:** Analyzes how high outage exposure hours correlate with overall energy utilization trends.

## Python Concepts Used

* **Data Structures:** Lists of nested dictionaries (`energy_data = []`) utilized to model multi-attribute customer records.
* **Control Flow:** Conditional statement blocks (`if`/`elif`/`else`) paired with logical operators (`and`, `or`) to execute categorization and threshold evaluations.
* **Iteration Logic:** `for` loops for portfolio-wide accumulation and metrics aggregation; `while` loops for application runtime control.
* **Modular Programming:** Custom Python functions (`def`) designed with explicit parameters and return statements to enforce clean code architecture.
* **Exception Handling:** `try-except` error-catching constructs to guarantee script stability during data validation checks.
* **Sorting Algorithms:** Bubble sort logic implemented via nested loops to order customer records by consumption volume.
---

## Project Workflow

1. **Data Initialization:** Embedding structured multi-attribute customer records into a Python list of dictionaries.
2. **Data Integrity and Validation Phase:** Running validation functions (`validate_data`) to check for anomalies, format inconsistencies, or missing attributes.
3. **Interactive Control Loop:** Initiating the terminal menu interface where users select specific analytical modules.
4. **Analytical Execution:** Passing data into dedicated modular functions (e.g., cost calculation, location breakdown, efficiency scoring) to compute results.
5. **Report and Summary Generation:** Outputting formatted text dashboards, console tables, and comprehensive summary statistics.

## Project Walkthrough

The application operates through a comprehensive 13-option interactive menu system backed by specialized analytical functions:
* **View All Records:**
Executes `view_all_records()`, printing formatted profiles for all 20 customer records detailing IDs, names, types, locations, and consumption metrics.

* **Consumption Metrics:**
Computes total portfolio consumption (**24,240 kWh**) and average customer consumption (**1,212 kWh**).

* **Extremes Identification:**
Scans the dataset to locate the highest consumer (*Industrial Works Ltd* - C016 at **3,600 kWh**) and the lowest consumer (*Hope Residence* - C015 at **190 kWh**).

* **Electricity Cost Calculation:**
Multiplies consumption by applicable tariffs to compute the total system electricity cost (**₦2,920,000**) and average cost per customer (**₦146,000**).

* **Customer Classification:**
Runs `classify_customers()` to sort users into Low, Medium, and High brackets and check for high-attention operational statuses.

* **Location Analysis Sub-Menu):**
Launches an interactive nested `while` loop allowing analysts to query total consumption across specific geographic locations (Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan) cross-referenced with specific customer types (Residential, SME, Industrial, Hospital, School, Agricultural) without exiting to the main menu.

* **Customer Type Analysis:**
Aggregates consumption across all six distinct industrial and domestic categories.

* **Monthly Analysis:**
Tracks demand progression across the 6-month evaluation timeline (January through June).

* **High-Attention Customers:**
Filters and displays accounts requiring immediate administrative review based on high usage and low renewable contribution.

* **Generate Summary:**
Executes the system's core summary report dashboard, aggregating key metrics, cost parameters, and operational counts into a clean, unified view.

* **Exit:**
Safely terminates the interactive runtime loop.

* **Advanced Supporting Modules:**
In addition to the menu items, the code includes custom scripts for Bubble Sort Top 10 Ranking, Energy Efficiency Scoring (grading conservation, clean energy, and outage resilience), Outage Impact Analysis, and Affordability Analysis relative to standard incomes.

## Result

* **Total & Average Consumption:** Total portfolio consumption reached **24,240 kWh** across 20 customer records, resulting in an average consumption of **1,212 kWh** per customer.
  
* **Total Electricity Cost:** The aggregate financial cost evaluated across all customer tariffs equaled **₦2,920,000**, with an average customer cost of **₦146,000**.
  
* **Geographic Distribution:** Lagos recorded the highest total consumption at **9,760 kWh**, followed by Abuja at **8,090 kWh**, and Ibadan at **6,390 kWh**.
  
* **Customer Type Load Breakdown:** Industrial users consumed **9,600 kWh**, Hospitals consumed **4,150 kWh**, SMEs used **4,050 kWh**, Agricultural users accounted for **3,580 kWh**, Schools used **1,640 kWh**, and Residential users totaled **1,220 kWh**. Industrial accounts drove nearly 40% of total consumption from just 3 records.
  
* **Monthly Consumption Trends:** Monthly totals fluctuated across the 6-month evaluation period (January: **2,580 kWh**; February: **1,940 kWh**; March: **6,050 kWh**; April: **2,200 kWh**; May: **3,750 kWh**; June: **7,720 kWh**)].
  
* **Outage vs Consumption Impact:** Consumers facing low outage exposure (<10 hours) accounted for 71% of consumption (**2,152 kWh** average), whereas high outage groups accounted for only 29% (**898.67 kWh** average) .

## Summary Report Dashboard
The system incorporates a dedicated summary report function (`generate_summary()`) that compiles the results of individual analytical modules into a clean terminal dashboard When invoked (via Menu Option 12), the report outputs:
* **Portfolio Totals:** Total Customers (**20**), Total Consumption (**24,240 kWh**), and Average Consumption (**1,212 kWh**).
* **Financial Overview:** Total Electricity Cost (**₦2,920,000**) and Average Cost (**₦146,000**).
* **Consumer Extremes:** Highest consumer ID (C016, Industrial Works Ltd, **3,600 kWh**) and lowest consumer ID (C015, Hope Residence, **190 kWh**).
* **Regional and Operational Highlights:** Highest consuming location (Lagos at **9,760 kWh**) and total count of customers needing operational attention (**4**).

## Key Findings
* **Heavy Industrial Concentration:** Industrial customers account for **9,600 kWh** of the total **24,240 kWh** portfolio consumption (roughly **40%**) derived from just **3** out of **20** customer records, confirming significant load concentration.
* **Balanced Regional Spread:** Demand across Lagos (**9,760 kWh**), Abuja (**8,090 kWh**), and Ibadan (**6,390 kWh**) is fairly distributed rather than monopolized by a single region.
* **Targeted High-Attention Flags (4 Customers):** Out of **9** customers classified in the High consumption band ($\ge1000$ kWh), exactly **4** accounts (Prime Plastics, Lakeside Hotel, Naija Foods Ltd, and Industrial Works Ltd) were flagged for having renewable shares below **15%**, proving that volume classification and attention rules perform distinct analytical functions.
* **Non-Linear Monthly Swings:** Monthly consumption fluctuates significantly across the **6** recorded months (peaking in June at **7,720 kWh** and bottoming in February at **1,940 kWh**), driven by which customer records fall into each month rather than a smooth seasonal curve.
